Lucky Draw Studio · Concept proposals

Twelve reveals for a state occasion

For the five slots after Name Reel Showtime, Red Packet, Departure Board, Gold Drum and Fortune Wheel. Each concept is built for a large hall and an audience of heads of state and dignitaries. The motion is slow and deliberate, the craft is high, and Sabah is at the heart of it. Round two (concepts 7–12) adds six new mechanisms, a sweep, a relay, merging drops, a bloom, a shadow and a pour, so no two draws need to look alike.

8–10 secondsLong enough to build anticipation, short enough to keep a formal programme moving.
No party effectsNo confetti cannons or casino flashes. The ending is a slow drift of gold dust and a held still frame.
Name as the heroThe winner's full name, titles included, set large in an engraved serif and readable from the back of the hall.
Sound with weightLow strings, bronze and silence instead of jingles. The audio carries the moment.
Key frame · the sun clears Low's Peak

Kinabalu Dawn

Recommended

The hall goes to night. Mount Kinabalu stands in silhouette, and sunrise becomes the draw.

  1. 0–3 sLayers of ridge and cloud drift past in deep blue. Names glimmer faintly like stars, then fade one by one.
  2. 3–7 sThe first ray strikes Low's Peak, and gold light runs down the ridgelines toward the viewer.
  3. 7–10 sThe sun clears the summit, and the winner's name is drawn across the sky in gold leaf, letter by letter.
Built with Three.js layered terrain + GSAP SplitTextLength ~10 s

Sabah's best-known landmark, recognisable to every visiting delegation. A calm and confident opener for the grand prize.

Key frame · rings of light after the third strike

The Agung

Recommended

A single great bronze gong, the agung of Sabah's ceremonies, hangs in a darkened hall under one spotlight.

  1. 0–2 sThe camera moves slowly in. The bronze shows its hammered surface and a warm reflection of the room.
  2. 2–7 sThree strikes, each with a real, deep resonance. Every strike sends rings of light across the face, carrying the names outward.
  3. 7–9 sOn the third strike the rings fold back to the central boss, and the winner's name rises from it, embossed in bronze.
Built with Three.js physical bronze + ripple shader + GSAPLength ~9 s

Rooted in Kadazan-Dusun and Murut ceremony, and it sounds as good as it looks. We would check the gong's form and decoration with a cultural adviser before the event.

Key frame · the chosen token lifts into the light

Crystal Ballot

Recommended

The draw is shown as plainly fair: a clear crystal urn holds an engraved gold token for every entrant.

  1. 0–3 sThe urn turns slowly. Tokens tumble through the glass, and the light catches the engraved names.
  2. 3–6 sThe hall dims to a single beam of light from above. One token rises out of the urn and into the beam.
  3. 6–9 sIt turns to face the audience, its engraving flares, and the full name settles above it.
Built with Three.js glass (transmission) + instanced tokens + GSAPLength ~9 s

At a high-profile event, being seen to be fair matters as much as the spectacle, and this concept shows the fairness.

Key frame · the seal is pressed

State Proclamation

The award is presented as a formal decree: an ivory scroll on gilded rollers, laid on a dark lacquered table.

  1. 0–3 sThe camera looks down on the sealed scroll. The ribbon slips loose and the scroll unrolls toward the viewer.
  2. 3–7 sA line of calligraphy writes itself in ink, stroke by stroke, forming the winner's name.
  3. 7–9 sA gold wax seal bearing the event emblem presses down beside the name with a soft, low thud.
Built with SVG/DOM + GSAP (DrawSVG-style ink, SplitText)Length ~9 s

The seal would use the organiser's own emblem, supplied by you. We won't reproduce the state crest or any other official insignia without permission. The key frame shows a placeholder monogram.

Key frame · every district converges on Kota Kinabalu · outline illustrative

Map of Light

The coastline of Sabah is drawn in gold on deep navy, and every entrant is a point of light somewhere in the state.

  1. 0–3 sThe outline traces itself from Kudat around to Tawau, and points of light appear across the districts.
  2. 3–7 sThreads of light run from every district toward the capital, braiding together as they travel.
  3. 7–9 sThey meet at Kota Kinabalu in one bright point, which opens out into the winner's name.
Built with WebGL particles + GSAPLength ~9 s

Brings the whole state into one moment. The final build would use an accurate state outline from public map data, not the sketch shown here.

Key frame · the beadwork parts like a curtain · motif illustrative

Pinakol

A tribute to Rungus beadwork: a full-screen geometric weave in the traditional black, white, red and yellow.

  1. 0–3 sThe weave builds bead by bead from the centre outward, in a rhythm of soft clicks.
  2. 3–6 sThe pattern ripples. Names surface in the motif and sink back again.
  3. 6–9 sThe weave parts like a curtain to reveal the winner, framed by a band of beads.
Built with Canvas/WebGL instanced beads + GSAPLength ~9 s

The most distinctly Sabahan of the six. Motifs carry meaning, so the patterns should be checked with a Rungus cultural adviser before they're used.

Round two · concepts 7–12
Key frame · the beam settles on one panel

Honour Wall

Shortlist

Every entrant is engraved on a dark wall, each in its own brass panel. A beam of light travels the wall and stops on one name, which steps forward to be read.

  1. 0–2 sThe wall comes up out of the dark. All the names are visible in their panels, so the whole field is seen before anything moves.
  2. 2–6 sA narrow beam sweeps the rows. It gathers pace, then eases down over the final row, so the stop is smooth rather than abrupt.
  3. 6–9 sThe lit panel lifts out of the wall and grows into the centre. The rest of the wall dims, and the name is set in gold leaf.
Built with DOM grid + GSAP (transforms and opacity only)Length ~9 s

Showing the whole field makes the fairness visible to guests. Very long entrant lists would need a paging or smaller-tile rule, and we would test that with the full list.

Key frame · the flame settles on the last lamp

Lamp Relay

Shortlist

A row of brass oil lamps runs away down the length of the hall. A single flame is passed along the row until it comes to rest on the winner's lamp.

  1. 0–2 sThe lamps stand unlit in a long line that recedes into the dark, like the hall itself.
  2. 2–6 sThe flame leaves the first lamp and travels the row. Each lamp catches with a soft bloom of light. The flame quickens in the middle and slows as it nears the end.
  3. 6–9 sThe flame settles on the last lamp, which brightens, and the winner's name rises above it in warm light.
Built with Three.js point-light glow, or Canvas 2D + GSAPLength ~9 s

Lighting a lamp is a familiar gesture at official openings, so it reads as ceremony rather than spectacle. The lamp style and lighting protocol should be confirmed with the organiser's protocol team.

Key frame · one drop remains on the glass

Rain Glass

Each entrant is a drop of rain on a dark window. The drops run down the glass and join one another until only one is left, carrying the winner's name.

  1. 0–3 sRain beads across the pane in the warm glow of a window light. Each drop carries a faint name.
  2. 3–7 sThe drops slide, meet and merge. The pane clears a little with each join, and the sound of rain softens.
  3. 7–9 sOne drop is left at the centre. It slows, and the name comes into focus through the water.
Built with Canvas 2D droplet physics (clamped dt) + GSAPLength ~9 s

The winner is still chosen first in core.js, so the merging is choreographed to finish on that drop. The selection does not change; only the picture is staged to match it.

Key frame · the last petal turns back

Orchid Bloom

A single orchid in a dark room. Its petals open one by one, and the winner's name rests in the centre of the flower as the last petal turns back.

  1. 0–3 sA closed bud sits in soft light and breathes almost imperceptibly.
  2. 3–7 sThe petals unfold in turn from the base outward. Each one has a small overshoot and settles before the next begins.
  3. 7–9 sThe flower stills. Its centre lifts toward the audience, and the name is set into it.
Built with SVG petals + GSAP timeline (rotate and scale only)Length ~9 s

Mount Kinabalu's slopes are known for their orchids, so the image ties the draw to the state's own landscape. It is the quietest concept on the board and the most organic.

Key frame · the shadow stops on one name

Sundial

A large brass sundial stands in the hall with every entrant's name engraved around its rim. A shadow sweeps the dial in the warm light of late afternoon and stops on one name.

  1. 0–2 sThe dial catches the warm light, and the engraved names around the rim can be read in the glow.
  2. 2–7 sThe shadow of the gnomon turns slowly, builds to cruise speed, then eases across the names and slows to a stop.
  3. 7–9 sThe winner's engraving fills with gold light, and the name rises beside the dial.
Built with Three.js (one warm key light, shadow on a dial model) + GSAPLength ~9 s

Patient and time-honoured, so it suits an opening session. The shadow is a set piece under a fixed warm light rather than a true sun simulation, and the dial is for drawing names, not for keeping time.

Key frame · the metal fills the letters

Foundry

The winner's name is cast in gold in front of the audience. Molten metal fills the mould, cools to a gold plate, and is lifted into the light.

  1. 0–2 sA furnace glow rises in the dark, and the crucible tilts toward the mould.
  2. 2–6 sA bright stream of metal runs into the mould and fills the lettering, and the glow spreads across the plate.
  3. 6–9 sThe metal cools from orange to gold, a single resonant note sounds, and the cast plate is lifted from the mould with the name upon it.
Built with Canvas/WebGL shader for the molten flow + GSAPLength ~9 s

The cast plate could be the keepsake each winner takes home, which turns the moment into part of the prize. Its metal sound is close to The Agung's, so the two would not be used in the same session.

Suggested line-up

  • Recommended for the five open slots: Kinabalu Dawn, The Agung and Crystal Ballot, plus two of State Proclamation, Map of Light and Pinakol.
  • Grand prize: Kinabalu Dawn, the most cinematic of the six.
  • Needs input from you: the event emblem (for Proclamation) and a cultural adviser's sign-off (for The Agung and Pinakol).
  • Round two shortlist: Honour Wall (it shows the whole field) and Lamp Relay (the most ceremonial). Foundry is a strong keepsake option. Lamp Relay needs the protocol team's sign-off on the lamp style.
  • All twelve use the studio's existing flow: the same names list, fair draw, winners log, keyboard and clicker controls, and they run fully offline.
